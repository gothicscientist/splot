## MODE 4 — HISTORY EXPLAINED (why/how did X happen)

**Toggles:**

- **Dark History:** villain bios, punishments, weird deaths
- **War Tactics:** formations, ambushes, sieges
- **"How to become X"** second-person career path, e.g. "How to be POPE in 55 Years", 21.8M

**Purpose.** Make a weird or surprising historical fact make sense through its causes. Comedy first, accuracy always.

**Input.** An event, custom, ruler, era or weird question ("why did the French hate potatoes", "how did kings avoid poison", "why did soldiers fight in lines").

**Research steps:**

1. Wikipedia plus 2 authoritative sources (Britannica, museum or university pages, a well-cited book).
2. Find the **modern hook angle**: paradox, superlative, or a "you" framing.
3. Lay out the causal chain in 3–5 steps; collect dates, numbers and one primary-source quote if possible.
4. **Myth check.** Popular history Shorts are full of myths; Historically ends one video by busting one: "pilots weren't chained up in their cockpits. That one is a post-war myth."
5. Dark toggle: no gore close-ups; imply rather than show; age-safe wording.

**Script template, 45 s (~155 words at ~205 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | HOOK | Absurd-sounding claim ("Being a king is a very hungry job.") | Character in period costume plus one visual gag (crown + empty plate) |
| 2–5 | BRIDGE | "I know, sounds weird, but hear me out." / "Let me explain." | Character shrug; title card with year |
| 5–15 | STAKES | Why the problem mattered ("everyone kind of just wants to kill you… poison") | Map or establishing shot; threat icons; stamped date |
| 15–30 | ATTEMPTS | 2–3 escalating solutions, each funnier or more extreme (food taster → slow poison → locked kitchens) | One scene per attempt; reaction faces; "FAIL" stamps |
| 30–40 | THE REAL ANSWER / TWIST | The most extreme true fact ("took poison daily to build immunity") | Hero moment: slow zoom, music hit |
| 40–45 | KICKER | Consequence or myth-bust or irony ("…and then the French Revolution started.") + optional "Subscribe." | Ironic final image; loopable |

**75 s extension:** add a "first you have to…" step ladder (the career-path variant), a second era or region comparison, and a myth vs reality split. Historically's winners run 59–95 s at 179–230 wpm.

**Hook templates:**

1. "[People / role] [hated / feared / were obsessed with] [harmless thing]." ("French people hate potatoes, or at least used to." — 21.9M)
2. "Here's how to [become X] in [N years]. First, you have to [absurd prerequisite]." ("…First, you have to be born a man. So, that eliminates about 50% of your chances." — 21.8M)
3. "Why did [soldiers / people] [do obviously dumb thing]?" ("Who were easier to hit, these guys or these funny guys?" — line formations, 20M)

Dark toggle:

- "[Famous person] was way more [evil / gross] than you were taught." ("Christopher Columbus was way more evil than you were taught." — History Dude, 11.3M)
- Title pattern "[Name] Was HORRIFYING!" (Pablo Escobar 21M; Henry VIII 13.3M; Blackbeard 13.2M)

**Best art styles.**

- 2D flat vector characters on maps (Historically)
- Countryballs (7.25M median)
- Cutout caricature heads on oil paintings (History Dude, curiousmotions)
- Stick figures in a 3D period diorama (reference style)

**Visual conventions.**

- Year stamp on every scene change; map establishing shot with a red-arrow/inset circle (Historically).
- Period costume swaps on one recurring character.
- Bold one-word captions ("HERE"); stamps (BANNED, FAILED).
- Troop arrows for tactics; before/after split for myths.

**Voice and audio.**

- Cheeky, conversational, fast (200–230 wpm). Dark toggle: same speed, a lower and drier tone.
- Music: playful pizzicato/baroque; dark toggle uses low strings and a heartbeat pulse.

**Ending.** Ironic kicker, myth-bust, or a one-word "Subscribe." (Historically does this).

**Common mistakes.**

- Textbook chronology.
- Too many dates.
- A neutral biography: "biography" Shorts search median is only 23.5K.
- Repeating internet myths.
- Gore.

**Retention.**

- Comedic asides ("Sorry, ladies.").

**Endings.** Ironic consequence: "…and then the French Revolution started." / "…took poison each day in small quantities… Subscribe."

**Mistakes.**
