## MODE 5 — BACK THEN (everyday life across eras)

**Purpose.** Take one everyday activity or object (shaving, toilets, dentists, dating, food, shoes, entertainment) and time-travel it through 4–6 eras in the second person, ending on a modern punchline that turns on the viewer.

Variants:

- **"Would you survive / get rich…"** — a "Day 1 / Day 2…" business or survival sim in an era. Example: "Would you become rich by selling burgers in ancient Rome?" (4.25M)
- **"How long would [person] survive in every era"** (MrFineWayne, 2.7M)
- **"Origin of [thing]"** — invention story, e.g. Rom "How Nutella Was Invented" (16M)
- **"Ancient humans…"** — the stickman long-form trend ("What Did Ancient Humans Actually Do All Day?", 18M long-form)

**Input.** An everyday thing or activity, or a premise ("selling coffee in ancient Greece").

**Research steps:**

1. Pick 4–6 eras with one concrete, documented method each: Stone Age → Egypt → Rome → Medieval → 1700s → Today. Use 2+ sources per era (museum, university, encyclopedia).
2. For each era: the tool or material, one gross or funny detail, one number.
3. For the sim variant, build a day-by-day ladder: setup → success → historical obstacle (law, plague, tax, material) → twist moral.
4. Myth check. Many "back then" facts online are false — reject anything without a source.

**Script template, 45 s (~150 words at ~200–210 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | "How did people [do X] before [invention] even existed?" | Modern object crossed out; the avatar shrugs |
| 3–12 | ERA 1 | "Stone Age, 100,000 years ago. Your [tool] is a [absurd tool]." + why it mattered ("you're not grooming, you're surviving") | Era title card with rolling year odometer; avatar in costume using the tool |
| 12–20 | ERA 2 | Famous-person cameo ("Julius Caesar has his beard plucked with tweezers") | New diorama; cameo character reacting |
| 20–28 | ERA 3 | Weird method from another culture or region | New diorama; close-up of the material |
| 28–36 | ERA 4 | The most dangerous or funny era ("1700s… one sneeze from your barber…") | Danger close-up; comic beat |
| 36–45 | TODAY + PUNCHLINE | Modern tech turned into a joke at the viewer's expense | Modern bathroom/kitchen; avatar deadpan; loop to the first frame |

**75 s extension:** 6–7 eras, or the sim structure. Sim structure: Day 1 setup (8 s), Day 2 boom (8 s), Day 5 obstacle (10 s), Day 10 pivot (10 s), Day 30 twist moral ("The burger was never the product…"). originico runs 66–71 s; MrFineWayne 42–73 s.

**Hook templates:**

1. "How did people [do X] before [invention] even existed?" (originico: shaving 12.6M; shampoo 4.0M. MrFineWayne: glasses 3.8M)
2. "Would you become rich by selling [modern product] in [era]?" (4.25M)
3. "How long would [famous person] survive in every era of history?" (2.7M)

Also:

- "Have you ever wondered how [object] went from this to this?" (Hoodie Guy, shoes, 20.0M)
- Title pattern "'[Activity]' Back Then…" (Hoodie Guy, 35M)

**Best art styles.**

- **Stick figure / caveman avatar time-travelling through 3D toon dioramas** — the best fit with the reference style
- Flat 2D with a recurring character
- Stylised 3D (Rom, Zack D)

**Visual conventions.**

- One recurring "you" character, re-costumed every era.
- Era card with a year odometer on every era change.
- Close-up of the tool or material.
- Reaction shot per era.
- Instrument swap in the music per era (bone flute → lyre → lute → harpsichord → synth).
- The final modern shot mirrors the first frame.

**Voice and audio.** Deadpan comedic, second person, fast (200–240 wpm), with short fragment sentences ("No cream, no antiseptic."). Music: era stingers.

**Ending.** A punchline about today ("…still can't handle your…"), or a moral twist for the sim variant.

**Common mistakes.**

- Third-person textbook tone (use "you").
- Too few eras or no escalation.
- Unsourced myths ("medieval people never bathed").
- No modern payoff.

- Universal relatability: everyone shaves, eats and dates.
- Gross-out and pain humour.
- Second-person time travel.
- A modern punchline pays off the viewer.
- Easy to make into a series (each object is a new episode).

**Verbatim hooks:**

1. "How did people shave before razors even existed? Stone Age, 100,000 years ago. Your razor is a clam shell."
2. "Would you become rich by selling burgers in ancient Rome? Day one, you show up in Rome with beef, a grill, and secret sauce."
3. "How did people wash their hair before shampoo? Ancient Egypt, you don't wash your hair, you shave it off."
4. "How did people fix bad vision before glasses even existed? Stone Age, you cannot see past 10 ft…"
5. "How long would Socrates survive in every era of history? 10,000 BC, Socrates wakes up in the Stone Age."
6. "Have you ever wondered how shoes went from this to this?"
7. "Kim Jong-un's father wanted to bring fast food to North Korea without admitting it came from the evil West."
8. "This is a day in the life of a peasant. I wake up at dawn… But first, ale." (content_machine, 9.3M — the only day-in-life outlier)

Interview hooks:

- "Excuse me, sir. I heard you can fight off predators with your ass."
- "Hey, you want to see how I get free child care?"
- "Dude, I'm never finding a girlfriend. Just look at my recessed jaw."
- "Excuse me, sir. Who are you and what do you do for a living?"

**Pattern.** A "how did people X before Y" question, then era-by-era in second person. Interview variant: a polite opener, then deflection, then a running gag.

**Length, pace, shots.**

- 63–73 s at 190–240 wpm.
- The Hoodie Guy storyboard showed a visual change in 61% of 1-second samples: a new shot roughly every 1.6 s or faster, alternating host / creature close-up / two-shot / chalkboard.

**Retention.**

- Era cards.
- Escalating pain.
- Running gags ("But what about your ass?").
- Payoff demonstration.

**Endings.** A modern punchline aimed at the viewer, or a moral twist: "The burger was never the product. You got rich because you gave people something they didn't know they wanted."

**Mistakes.** Third person; unsourced myths; a static narrator with no character.
