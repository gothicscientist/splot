## MODE 8 — PROBLEM → SOLUTION (app / product marketing)

**Purpose.** Deliver a genuinely useful explainer about the viewer's problem (why it happens and how to fix it). The product appears late, in-world, as the easy way to apply the fix. This mode optimises for downloads, not views, but **views drive downloads**, so it must stand on its own as content.

**Input (required):**

- Product name and what it does
- Target user
- The user's core problem
- 2–3 real features that map to the fix
- Proof points (only true ones)
- CTA text and link destination
- Brand colour/logo
- Forbidden claims (medical/financial/legal)

**Research steps:**

1. Find the mechanism behind the problem. Examples: variable rewards / Skinner box for doomscrolling; sleep pressure and blue light for insomnia; the spacing effect for forgetting.
2. Get 1–2 credible sources or stats for the mechanism.
3. Mine the user's own words from reviews, Reddit and comments. Use them in the hook and the relatable scene.
4. Map one fix → one product feature; prepare an honest phrasing.
5. Compliance pass: no guaranteed outcomes, no fake testimonials, disclose sponsorship where required.

**Script template, 45 s (~150 words):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | Name the viewer's pain + a surprise ("Why you can't stop scrolling — and it's not your fault.") | "You" avatar in the relatable moment (2 a.m., phone glow); bold 2-word caption |
| 3–12 | MECHANISM | The hidden cause, explained simply (Skinner-box experiment → slot machine → feed) | Diagram or cutaway: brain/reward loop, lab rat lever, slot reels morphing into a feed |
| 12–22 | CONSEQUENCE | What it costs you, with a number (hours, sleep, money) | Clock or counter spinning; avatar exhausted |
| 22–32 | THE FIX | 1 actionable, value-first principle (break the loop: friction, schedules, replacement reward) | The avatar applies the fix in-world; before/after split |
| 32–41 | PRODUCT AS TOOL | "The easiest way to do this is [product]: it [feature mapped to the fix]." | The app appears as a diegetic device in the toon world (phone or object in the avatar's hand); a 2–3 s real UI insert is allowed |
| 41–45 | SOFT CTA + LOOP | One-line soft CTA ("[Product], link in bio") + a callback to the hook moment, now solved | Hook scene replayed, resolved; small logo end card |

Placement rule: the product is introduced after about 70% of runtime. Operator evidence:

- Jake Castillo (Cal AI co-founder): put the app on slide 4–5 of 5–10, "let the story earn the attention first", and optimise for views.
- facelessfarms: the best campaign content kept the app mention light and put the CTA in the caption.
- rossark0: app icon around the middle or end.

**75 s extension.** Use 2 mechanisms or a myth-bust, a 3-step fix (one step uses the product), and a mini-case ("I tried this for 7 days…") before the CTA.

**Hook templates:**

1. "Why you can't [stop X / sleep / focus] (it's not your fault)." (G Logics "Why You Can't Stop Scrolling: The Skinner Box Explained", 26.7M)
2. "[Authority] [credential]. These are the [N] [rules / tips] [specific outcome]." (facelessfarms: "My dad worked homicide for 30 years. These are the rules he made every girl in our family memorize." — 80M views across a $40k campaign)
3. "How to trick your brain into [desired behaviour]." (Zack D "How To Trick Your Brain To Stop Procrastinating", 6.4M)

Also:

- "[Condition] explained in 60 seconds." (21.8M)
- "Your [brain / body] does [weird thing] every time you [action]."

**Best art styles.**

- The SAME recurring avatar character as the brand's organic channel (series recognition)
- Stick figure in a 3D toon world, or flat 2D
- Brain/body cutaways
- The app UI only as a short insert

**Visual conventions.**

- Problem scene → mechanism diagram → consequence counter → fix in-world → product in-hand → resolved callback.
- Never open on the logo or the UI.

**Voice and audio.**

- Empathetic peer, not an announcer; about 190–210 wpm.
- Music goes from tense or lo-fi to uplifting at the fix.

**Benchmarks to set expectations:**

- Influencer UGC: about 5 downloads per 1,000 views (Cal AI benchmark).
- Slideshow farms: about 1 download per 2,000 views.
- About 90% of slideshows stay under 5K views. Volume and multiple accounts matter (Jake Castillo, Oct 2026).

**Common mistakes.**

- Making a video ABOUT the app ("nobody wants to watch an ad" — Peef).
- A hard CTA on screen.
- The product appearing in the first half.
- Unverifiable health or money claims.
- Generic advice with no mechanism.
- Posting once a day from one account (volume is the lever).

## Evidence and real winners for this mode

## 2.8 Money and business stories

**Why it wins:** aspiration, "how they trick you", and genius-hack curiosity. RPM is high on long-form, but Shorts views are lower on average:

| Search query | Median |
|---|---|
| "how this company makes money" | 18K |
| "business story" | 303K |
| "billionaire story" | 19K |

It works best as a Case File (scams, laundering, corporate blunders) or as How It Works ("How Food Companies Make You Eat More", Zack D 7.8M).

**Accounts and videos.**

| Channel / video | Views | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| @Groomforwealth "When Netflix Accidentally Taught Millions How to Launder Money" | 19.8M | Movie clip + narration; channel median 217K |
| @CuriousWolff "Candies Companies REGRET Making" | 15M / 11M / 6.7M / 6.0M | Series |

| @Corporate_Tom "Nepotism Explained" | 341K | "for kids" framing, 143–161 wpm |
| @27_crew "He Got Rich Turning Trash into THIS!" | 78M | |

**Verbatim hooks:**

- "What did you do with your money? I cleaned it. Okay. Money laundering 101."
- "The inventor of instant ramen believed his noodles could cure world hunger…"
- "100 years of donuts in 1 minute."
- "Nepotism explained for kids. Meet Joe and Hero."

**Verdict.** Not a standalone mode. Route it to Case File, How It Works, or Problem→Solution.

**Operator evidence (X posts, self-reported, 2026):**

- **Peef (@PeefTwt, 13 Nov 2026):** "$700+ in one day, 100% organic". Advice: "don't make a video about your app… make a video about your users' problem, teach them something useful, and your app shows up at the end as THE solution." 192 likes, 377 bookmarks.
- **Jake Castillo (19 Oct 2026, 26.3K views):**
  - Cal AI influencer benchmark: 5 downloads per 1,000 views; slideshow farms about 1 per 2,000.
  - Put the app on slide 4–5; be as topical as possible.
  - About 90% of slideshows stay under 5K views; a few per account hit 50K–500K.
- **@facelessfarms (27 Aug 2026):**
  - A $40k campaign at $1.75 CPM produced 80M views versus about 22M expected.
  - 7–8% engagement; authority-list hook.
  - "The best performing content in this campaign did NOT put a hard CTA in the video itself."
- **@jackfriks:** 30,000 downloads in a month from 6 videos/day (organic).
- **@rossark0:** the Symmetry app at 200K downloads and about $100K revenue per month via 2–3-slide TikTok slideshows across many accounts; "show your app icon around the middle or end".
