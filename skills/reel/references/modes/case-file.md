## MODE 1 — CASE FILE (true story)

**Purpose.** Retell ONE real, surprising, verifiable event as a tight cause→effect story that ends on a twist or consequence. Sub-types:

- Heist or robbery
- Scam, con or fraud
- Hack or crypto theft
- Escape or manhunt ("how they caught him")
- Spy or military mission
- Rescue or survival
- Corporate blunder ("Nintendo regrets suing this guy")
- Disaster explained
- "The only person who…"

This mode fits the user's reference (a $6B laptop heist) and the reconstruction style of @ProfessorOfHow.

**Input.**

- An event, person or company plus an optional angle. For example: "Binance $6.3B hack", "Baker Street heist", "man who survived two atomic bombs".
- Optional flags: tone (deadpan, thriller, comedic) and length (45 or 75 s).

**Research steps (the agent must do all):**

1. Identify the canonical event: date, place, actors, amounts. Get at least 3 independent sources (Wikipedia plus 2 primary or press sources: court records, official statements, major outlets). Store citations in `videos/<slug>/research.md`.
2. Build a timeline of 8–12 facts. Mark each fact *confirmed*, *alleged* or *disputed*. For example, the Binance "single laptop" framing is contested; Binance describes a months-long campaign across 3 engineers.
3. Extract the **hook number**, the single most absurd figure (dollar amount, minutes, ratio), and the **twist**: the small mistake, hidden accomplice, denial, or ironic consequence.
4. Find one physical or visual detail per beat: the object, the place, the screen, the vehicle.
5. Safety pass:
   - Hedge allegations ("allegedly", "according to the FBI").
   - Don't name private individuals or minors.
   - No graphic gore. Avoid victim-centred murder cases, which underperform on Shorts (search median 19K) and carry policy risk.

**Script template, 45 s (~150 words):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen (must visualize) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | Paradox question or outcome-first line built on the hook number ("How could one single laptop cost 6 billion dollars?") | The hero object huge in frame with an animated price/number tag; protagonist reacts |
| 3–8 | SETUP | Who / where / when in one line, plus why it mattered | Location pin on a mini-map, year stamp, establishing shot of the organisation |
| 8–20 | THE PLAN | 3 escalating steps at 3–4 s each ("fake recruiters → a coding test → malware on a work laptop") | One scene per step: chat-UI prop, file download, laptop infection glow; arrows connect the steps |
| 20–28 | THE TURN | Clock or stakes ("20 minutes later, the money was gone") | Countdown/stopwatch overlay, balance draining, alarm-red grade |
| 28–38 | TWIST / UNRAVEL | How it was traced, or the dumb mistake ("12,000 wallets… the FBI traced it to North Korea… which denied everything") | Red-string graph splitting into wallet nodes; investigator figure; flag stamp; "DENIED" stamp |
| 38–45 | AFTERMATH + KICKER | Consequence number plus ironic last line or open loop | Freeze on the kicker number; callback to the hook object (loop) |

**75 s extension (~250 words):**

- Insert "But let's start from the beginning" at about 8 s, then a 10 s backstory (who the actors were, why the target was vulnerable).
- Make the plan 5 steps instead of 3.
- Add a **mid-video re-hook** at 35–40 s: "But that's not even the crazy part."
- Use a 2-step aftermath (legal consequence, then the irony).

**Hook templates:**

1. "How could one single [object] cost [absurd number]?"
2. "[N] [people] [did X] and only [one] [survived / got caught / got away]." Source: "A four-man Navy SEAL team was ambushed in the mountains of Afghanistan and only one of them made it out alive."
3. "In [year], [someone] [did something ordinary]… and [huge consequence]." Variants: "In 2012, an anonymous user on 4chan found an old grenade in the forest." / "Back in the 1970s, a group of totally sane people checked themselves into insane asylums…"

Spares:

- "[Person] got caught because of [tiny mistake]." Zack D's Baker Street ending: "they got caught because one of them used their real name."
- A cold-open action line: "A bomb was strapped around the neck of a pizza delivery man in 2003."

**Best art styles.**

- Stick figures in a 3D toon world (the reference)
- 2.5D ink-outline characters in a painted 3D town
- Stylised 3D (Zack D / Professor Of How)
- Paper-cutout documents and evidence boards for the investigation beats

**Visual conventions.**

- Every number is an animated counter.
- Every place is a map pin with a year stamp.
- Digital crime uses diegetic UI props (chat bubbles, wallet graphs, terminals).
- "Two sides" moments use split screen.
- Captures and reveals get a slam-zoom plus SFX hit; music drops out for about 0.5 s before the twist.

**Voice and audio.**

- Calm, deadpan documentary narrator with one or two dry asides. Zack D heists run about 160 wpm; Pezle and theartofwarrr run 220–249 wpm. Default 190–210.
- Music: low ticking pulse and synth bass; build to the turn, cut at the twist, soft sting on the kicker.

**Ending.** Kicker number plus irony, or an open mystery ("…and to this day, no one knows what happened to him." — Pezle). A loop back to the hook object is optional.

**Common mistakes.**

- Opening with background instead of the outcome.
- More than 2 named actors.
- Unverified numbers.
- Showing violence instead of implying it.
- No twist, i.e. a story that ends where the viewer expected.

## 2.1 True story — Case File (heists, scams, hacks, spies, missions, disasters)

**Why it wins.**

- A real outcome is a stronger curiosity gap than fiction ("this actually happened").
- Outcome-first framing creates a "how?" loop.
- Numbers make it shareable.
- The twist or comeuppance gives a moral payoff.
- Highly rewatchable, because the clues land differently once you know the end.

**Hook patterns.**

- **Cold-open action:** subject + vivid verb + year.
- **Outcome-first:** "…and only one made it out".
- **Normal act → huge consequence.**
- **Paradox price question.**

**Length, pace, shots.**

- 3D heist cuts: 42–48 s at about 160 wpm.
- Narrated 2D/3D story cuts: 44–75 s at 220–250 wpm.
- Professor Of How: 80–86 s.

**Retention devices.**

- Ticking clock.
- "You see, in 2005…" rewind after an outcome-first hook (theartofwarrr).
- Escalation in threes.
- Twist reveal ("this man had actually been involved in the plan").
- Open mystery ending (Pezle: "to this day, no one knows what happened to him").

**Endings.** Gut-punch consequence: "…the device detonated, killing him." / "they got caught because one of them used their real name."

**Mistakes.**

- Context-first openings.
- Graphic violence.
- Unhedged allegations.
- Too many names.
- Murder-victim stories (low median and policy risk).
