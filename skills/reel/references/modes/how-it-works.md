## MODE 3 — HOW IT WORKS (mechanism / how it's made / why does…)

**Purpose.** Open up an everyday object, process, product, body function or technical concept and show the hidden mechanism one part at a time. It ends on a "so that's why…" plus a surprising limit or fact.

Sub-types:

- How X works (silencer, inhaler, elevator, ATM)
- How X is made (agarwood, chips, glass)
- Why does your body… (cry, hiccup, vomit, splinter pain)
- How X makes money (business-model version)
- Mistakes you should know (safety list; Zeck Felms)

**Input.** Object, process or concept, plus the viewer's likely level (kid, general, nerd).

**Research steps:**

1. Collect manufacturer or engineering docs, patents, textbooks and medical references (NHS/NIH) — 2–4 sources.
2. Reduce the mechanism to 3–5 causal steps (input → hidden part → transformation → output).
3. Find the **hidden or violent or weird step** for the hook. Example: "A red hot iron is pushed into the tree, wounding it on purpose."
4. Find one everyday analogy and one real number (pressure, speed, temperature, price).
5. Verify safety: no weapon-building or drug-synthesis detail. Keep weapons at the conceptual level.

**Script template, 45 s (~130–150 words; calm 3D style ~170 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | Everyday action + hidden event ("Every time you press the elevator button, this happens on the roof.") or a weird process step | The everyday object in macro close-up, then a snap to the hidden location |
| 3–8 | GO INSIDE | "Here's what's actually happening" | X-ray/cutaway transition; exploded view begins |
| 8–30 | STEPS 1–3 | One part added per step, each 6–7 s, each with a big label | Labels and arrows showing flow (gas, current, liquid); one moving part at a time; slow-mo on the key moment |
| 30–38 | "SO" LINE | What the viewer feels in real life ("that's why it sounds like a pop, not a bang") | Back to human scale; the avatar reacts |
| 38–45 | SURPRISE + LOOP | Surprising limit, cost or myth-bust, phrased to loop to the opening | Callback to the opening shot with the new understanding (e.g. the label now visible) |

**75 s extension:** add a history-of-the-invention beat (5–8 s), a "wrong way vs right way" comparison, and a second myth-bust; or extend it into a full "how it's made" production line with 5–6 stations.

**Hook templates:**

1. "Every time you [everyday action], [hidden thing] happens [somewhere unseen]."
2. "[Expensive / ordinary product] is made by [violent / strange step]." ("A red hot iron is pushed into the tree, wounding it on purpose." — Zack D, 112.8M)
3. "Have you ever wondered why [body / everyday phenomenon]?" ("Have you ever wondered why water comes out of your eyeballs when you cry?" — Zack D, 42.9M)

Alternates:

- "If you [use X], watch out for these hidden dangers." (Zeck Felms, 16.2M)
- "This is how [X] actually works." (title pattern "How A Gun Silencer Actually Works", 52.5M)

**Best art styles.**

- Stylised 3D with cutaways (strongest evidence)
- Clean 2D infographic/diagram with a stick-figure operator
- Stick figure in a 3D toon machine-world for "factory" stories

**Visual conventions.**

- Exploded view, part labels, flow arrows, macro DOF.
- Show one part at a time; never a whole static diagram.
- Scale bars for tiny or huge parts.
- On-screen numbers.
- Per MakeViral's study of top 3D explainers, aim for about one new detail per second.

**Voice and audio.**

- Calm, precise, faintly amused narrator, 160–190 wpm. Zack D: 157–169 wpm, 28–48 s.
- Music: light curious bed; mechanical SFX (clicks, hiss, whoosh) synced to each part.

**Ending.** "So next time you [action], remember [mechanism]", or a loop line.

**Common mistakes.**

- Starting with history ("invented in 1888…").
- Too many parts.
- Diagrams without motion.
- Technical jargon without an analogy.
- Overlong: 3D how-it-works winners run 28–35 s.

## Evidence and real winners for this mode

## 2.3 How it works / how it's made / why does…

**Why it wins.**

- Everyday objects carry built-in relevance.
- The x-ray "inside view" is impossible to film, so animation has a monopoly here.
- Satisfying understanding ("so that's why").
- Very rewatchable because of the dense visuals.

**Verbatim hooks:**

1. "A red hot iron is pushed into the tree, wounding it on purpose." (112.8M)
2. "When you shoot a gun with a silencer on the end, the bullet travels down the barrel followed by high pressure gases…" (52.5M)
3. "Have you ever wondered why water comes out of your eyeballs when you cry?" (42.9M)
4. "He mixed silicone oil with boric acid attempting to create a synthetic rubber for the US military during World War II." (37.4M)
5. "If your child uses a moving walkway at the airport, watch out for these hidden dangers." (16.2M)
6. "If you get a splinter in your finger and look closely inside, it has gone under your skin and is pressing on the tiny nerves that feel pain." (1.5M)
7. MakeViral exemplar: "Every time you press the elevator button, this happens on the roof."

**Hook pattern.** Start mid-process with the strangest step, or start with an everyday action plus its hidden consequence.

**Length and pace.** 19–35 s; 157–213 wpm.

**Ending.** A "so…" line or loop. Zeck Felms uses an absurd escalating punchline as a comedy variant ("attach a rocket to the swing seat…").

**Mistakes.** History-first openings; static diagrams; too many parts.
