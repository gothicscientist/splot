# B — Winning faceless animated short-form formats (2025 → Nov 2026) and the content MODES to build

Research date: 13–14 Nov 2026. Target: a Claude Code skill suite that turns a one-line topic into a code-rendered (Remotion) vertical short.

## 0. TL;DR

**What the data says.** I sampled the newest 60 Shorts from 104 faceless or animated channels and took the median of each channel's latest 30. The strongest faceless animated channels right now are:

- **History and everyday-life comedy:** @HoodieGuyStories 11.5M (2D creature/host interviews and "back then" history; channel started May 2025), @KingCountryballAnimations 7.25M and @HeyHistorically 6.8M.
- **3D explainers and reconstructions:** @zackdfilms 6.4M (3D what-if / how / why) and @ProfessorOfHow 5.4M (3D true-story reconstructions).
- **Lore and maps:** @Ohara-the-Fox 3.55M (One Piece rating cards) and @GeoGlobeTales 3.3M (map explainers).

In every format, the median channel sits in the tens of thousands. Format alone doesn't produce views. A recurring character, a fast and joke-dense script, and a paradox-style hook do.

## 0.1 Global production rules (apply to every mode)

**1. Start speaking at frame 0.** In 94 of 97 transcribed winners the first spoken word lands within 0.25 s (95 of 97 within 1 s). No logo, no "in this video", no music-only intro. The first 3 seconds must contain the paradox, number or question **and** its visual.

**2. Length.**

| Platform / style | Length |
|---|---|
| 3D mechanism or what-if explainers | 20–48 s (Zack D 28–48 s; Nolas 20–24 s; Zeck Felms 18–23 s) |
| 2D narrative (history, lore, maps, interviews) | 55–75 s (Historically 59–95 s; Hoodie Guy 63–67 s; GeoGlobeTales 63–103 s; Professor Of How 80–86 s) |
| TikTok | Make the cut 61–75 s; Creator Rewards pays only for videos of 1 minute or more |
| YouTube Shorts | Allows up to 3 min. Since 31 Mar 2025 every start or replay counts as a view, so short loops pay. |

**3. Speed and shot rate.**

| Style | Speed |
|---|---|
| Calm 3D (Zack D) | 157–186 wpm |
| Comedic 2D (Historically, Hoodie Guy, originico) | 190–240 wpm |
| Fan or lore channels | 200–283 wpm |
| Avoid (except for slow "explained for kids" pieces) | below 150 wpm |

- About one new visual idea per spoken clause: a new shot or element every 1–2.5 s, and no still image held longer than 2.5 s.
- Captions are 1–3 words at a time, bold, centred in the lower-middle third.

**4. Hook archetypes that recur across winners.** Each mode below maps to these.

- (a) **Paradox statement:** "Being a king is a very hungry job."
- (b) **Outcome-first true story:** "…only one of them made it out alive."
- (c) **Vivid "If you…" hypothetical:** "If a megalodon swallowed you…"
- (d) **Question with an implied, surprising answer:** "How did people shave before razors even existed?"
- (e) **"This is X. But did you know…":** "This is Spain. But did you know a tiny piece of it belongs to Britain?"
- (f) **Superlative with a twist:** "the strongest Marine… someone no one would have expected"
- (g) **Dialogue cold open:** "Excuse me, sir. I heard you can fight off predators with your ass."

**5. Endings.**

- An ironic kicker line is the most common ending: "…and then the French Revolution started", or "…stand in America, look at Russia, and see tomorrow."
- Other endings: an open mystery ("to this day no one knows…"), a score reveal, a one-word "Subscribe." (Historically), "Follow X for more Y explained" (Silenthys), or a debate question.
- Loop-friendly endings that call back to the first image help replays.

**6. Platform policy guardrails.**

- **YouTube "inauthentic content" (since 15 Jul 2025):** templated or mass-produced repetitive content is not monetisable. Vary structure, characters, hooks and music between videos, and keep the research original.
- **Anime IP:** rights holders (Aniplex, Crunchyroll, Toei) are tightening enforcement in 2026. Never use clips or official art.
- **Instagram:** distribution of political content is reduced, so keep geography neutral.

# 1. RECOMMENDED MODES — build specs (ranked)

Word budgets assume about 200 wpm: a 45 s cut is about 145–155 words and a 75 s cut about 240–260 words. For calm 3D styles use about 175 wpm (45 s ≈ 130 words).

Every beat lists what MUST be on screen. The renderer should treat these as required shots.

## MODE 1 — CASE FILE (true story)

**Purpose.** Retell ONE real, surprising, verifiable event as a tight cause→effect story that ends on a twist or consequence. Sub-types:

- Heist or robbery
- Scam, con or fraud
- Hack or crypto theft
- Escape or manhunt ("how they caught him")
- Spy or military mission
- Rescue or survival
- Corporate blunder ("Nintendo regrets suing this guy")
- Disaster explained
- "The only person who…"

This mode fits the user's reference (a $6B laptop heist) and the reconstruction style of @ProfessorOfHow.

**Input.**

- An event, person or company plus an optional angle. For example: "Binance $6.3B hack", "Baker Street heist", "man who survived two atomic bombs".
- Optional flags: tone (deadpan, thriller, comedic) and length (45 or 75 s).

**Research steps (the agent must do all):**

1. Identify the canonical event: date, place, actors, amounts. Get at least 3 independent sources (Wikipedia plus 2 primary or press sources: court records, official statements, major outlets). Store citations at the top of SCRIPT.md.
2. Build a timeline of 8–12 facts. Mark each fact *confirmed*, *alleged* or *disputed*. For example, the Binance "single laptop" framing is contested; Binance describes a months-long campaign across 3 engineers.
3. Extract the **hook number**, the single most absurd figure (dollar amount, minutes, ratio), and the **twist**: the small mistake, hidden accomplice, denial, or ironic consequence.
4. Find one physical or visual detail per beat: the object, the place, the screen, the vehicle.
5. Safety pass:
   - Hedge allegations ("allegedly", "according to the FBI").
   - Don't name private individuals or minors.
   - No graphic gore. Avoid victim-centred murder cases, which underperform on Shorts (search median 19K) and carry policy risk.

**Script template, 45 s (~150 words):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen (must visualize) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | Paradox question or outcome-first line built on the hook number ("How could one single laptop cost 6 billion dollars?") | The hero object huge in frame with an animated price/number tag; protagonist reacts |
| 3–8 | SETUP | Who / where / when in one line, plus why it mattered | Location pin on a mini-map, year stamp, establishing shot of the organisation |
| 8–20 | THE PLAN | 3 escalating steps at 3–4 s each ("fake recruiters → a coding test → malware on a work laptop") | One scene per step: chat-UI prop, file download, laptop infection glow; arrows connect the steps |
| 20–28 | THE TURN | Clock or stakes ("20 minutes later, the money was gone") | Countdown/stopwatch overlay, balance draining, alarm-red grade |
| 28–38 | TWIST / UNRAVEL | How it was traced, or the dumb mistake ("12,000 wallets… the FBI traced it to North Korea… which denied everything") | Red-string graph splitting into wallet nodes; investigator figure; flag stamp; "DENIED" stamp |
| 38–45 | AFTERMATH + KICKER | Consequence number plus ironic last line or open loop | Freeze on the kicker number; callback to the hook object (loop) |

**75 s extension (~250 words):**

- Insert "But let's start from the beginning" at about 8 s, then a 10 s backstory (who the actors were, why the target was vulnerable).
- Make the plan 5 steps instead of 3.
- Add a **mid-video re-hook** at 35–40 s: "But that's not even the crazy part."
- Use a 2-step aftermath (legal consequence, then the irony).

**Hook templates:**

1. "How could one single [object] cost [absurd number]?"
2. "[N] [people] [did X] and only [one] [survived / got caught / got away]." Source: "A four-man Navy SEAL team was ambushed in the mountains of Afghanistan and only one of them made it out alive."
3. "In [year], [someone] [did something ordinary]… and [huge consequence]." Variants: "In 2012, an anonymous user on 4chan found an old grenade in the forest." / "Back in the 1970s, a group of totally sane people checked themselves into insane asylums…"

Spares:

- "[Person] got caught because of [tiny mistake]." Zack D's Baker Street ending: "they got caught because one of them used their real name."
- A cold-open action line: "A bomb was strapped around the neck of a pizza delivery man in 2003."

**Best art styles.**

- Stick figures in a 3D toon world (the reference)
- 2.5D ink-outline characters in a painted 3D town
- Stylised 3D (Zack D / Professor Of How)
- Paper-cutout documents and evidence boards for the investigation beats

**Visual conventions.**

- Every number is an animated counter.
- Every place is a map pin with a year stamp.
- Digital crime uses diegetic UI props (chat bubbles, wallet graphs, terminals).
- "Two sides" moments use split screen.
- Captures and reveals get a slam-zoom plus SFX hit; music drops out for about 0.5 s before the twist.

**Voice and audio.**

- Calm, deadpan documentary narrator with one or two dry asides. Zack D heists run about 160 wpm; Pezle and theartofwarrr run 220–249 wpm. Default 190–210.
- Music: low ticking pulse and synth bass; build to the turn, cut at the twist, soft sting on the kicker.

**Ending.** Kicker number plus irony, or an open mystery ("…and to this day, no one knows what happened to him." — Pezle). A loop back to the hook object is optional.

**Common mistakes.**

- Opening with background instead of the outcome.
- More than 2 named actors.
- Unverified numbers.
- Showing violence instead of implying it.
- No twist, i.e. a story that ends where the viewer expected.

## MODE 2 — WHAT IF (hypothetical consequence chain)

**Purpose.** Take a vivid "what if / what happens if / could X really…" question and walk the viewer through real consequences in time or severity order, ending on a twist or survival trick.

**Input.** A question or subject, e.g. "what if you were swallowed by a whale", "what if humans had hummingbird hearts", "could samurai armor stop bullets". Optional framing: second person ("you") or planet-scale.

**Research steps:**

1. Find 2–4 reputable science sources (NASA, NIH/NHS, university pages, textbooks, reviews).
2. List 4–6 consequences on a timeline (T+1 s, 1 min, 1 h, 1 day, 1 year) or a severity ladder. Attach a number to each.
3. Find the **counter-intuitive turn**:
   - "But if you're lucky…"
   - "It actually depends…"
   - A real historical case, such as musket tests on samurai armor.
4. Flag speculation as speculation. No dangerous how-to instructions.

**Script template, 45 s (~135 words at 180 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | HOOK | "If [vivid scenario], [immediate visceral result]." ("If a megalodon swallowed you, its huge teeth can tear your body in seconds.") | The "you" avatar at the instant of danger; extreme close-up |
| 2–8 | STEP 1 — instant | First physical effect, with a number | Time counter "T+00:01"; cutaway/x-ray of body or object |
| 8–20 | STEPS 2–3 — escalation | Each step worse, each a new angle | Camera moves inside (stomach, atmosphere, armor layers); danger meter rising |
| 20–32 | STEP 4 — extreme | The worst or largest-scale outcome | Pull-back wide shot, city/planet scale; number counter |
| 32–40 | TWIST | "But if you're lucky…" / "it actually depends…" / real-world case | Lighting change; the survival trick shown step by step |
| 40–45 | KICKER | One-line ironic or punchy close that loops to the start | Callback to the first frame |

**75 s extension:**

- Use explicit chapters ("After 1 hour… After 1 day… After 1 year…").
- Add a second-order consequence (economy, animals, society) and a real precedent.
- Add a re-hook at about 35 s: "And this is where it gets weird."

**Hook templates:**

1. "If you [scenario], [shocking result] in [N seconds]."
2. "What would happen if [everyone / Earth / your body] [change]?" (Hoodie Guy "What If Humans Disappeared?" 46M: "In the first 24 hours, lights all around the world would start to shut down…")
3. "Could [object] really [stop / survive / turn into] [extreme]?" (Nolas: "If you stepped into a lake and felt yourself slowly turning to stone, could that actually be real?")

**Variants.**

- *Choice* ("Pick a Superpower With a Terrible Curse", NYKentertain 3.0M).
- *Deal* ("Tony Stark offers you a deal. Hide the Reality Stone anywhere within a mile of Ancient Rome…", MrFineWayne 4.1M).

**Best art styles.**

- Stylised 3D with cutaways (the Zack D / Nolas look)
- Stick figure as the "you" avatar in a 3D toon world
- Flat 2D diagram overlays for numbers

**Visual conventions.** The "you" avatar is on screen in every beat, alongside a running T+ clock, x-ray or cross-section shots, a danger or health meter, and a scale pull-back at the extreme beat.

**Voice and audio.**

- Calm, curious, slightly ominous; 175–200 wpm.
- Ambient suspense bed; a riser into the extreme beat; a release into the twist.

**Ending.** A punchy close or a loop. 3D what-if winners in the sample run only 20–31 s, so keep the 45 s cut tight.

**Common mistakes.**

- Fantasy with no science.
- A linear list without escalation.
- No twist.
- Running past 50 s on a single-mechanism premise.

## MODE 3 — HOW IT WORKS (mechanism / how it's made / why does…)

**Purpose.** Open up an everyday object, process, product, body function or technical concept and show the hidden mechanism one part at a time. It ends on a "so that's why…" plus a surprising limit or fact.

Sub-types:

- How X works (silencer, inhaler, elevator, ATM)
- How X is made (agarwood, chips, glass)
- Why does your body… (cry, hiccup, vomit, splinter pain)
- How X makes money (business-model version)
- Mistakes you should know (safety list; Zeck Felms)

**Input.** Object, process or concept, plus the viewer's likely level (kid, general, nerd).

**Research steps:**

1. Collect manufacturer or engineering docs, patents, textbooks and medical references (NHS/NIH) — 2–4 sources.
2. Reduce the mechanism to 3–5 causal steps (input → hidden part → transformation → output).
3. Find the **hidden or violent or weird step** for the hook. Example: "A red hot iron is pushed into the tree, wounding it on purpose."
4. Find one everyday analogy and one real number (pressure, speed, temperature, price).
5. Verify safety: no weapon-building or drug-synthesis detail. Keep weapons at the conceptual level.

**Script template, 45 s (~130–150 words; calm 3D style ~170 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | Everyday action + hidden event ("Every time you press the elevator button, this happens on the roof.") or a weird process step | The everyday object in macro close-up, then a snap to the hidden location |
| 3–8 | GO INSIDE | "Here's what's actually happening" | X-ray/cutaway transition; exploded view begins |
| 8–30 | STEPS 1–3 | One part added per step, each 6–7 s, each with a big label | Labels and arrows showing flow (gas, current, liquid); one moving part at a time; slow-mo on the key moment |
| 30–38 | "SO" LINE | What the viewer feels in real life ("that's why it sounds like a pop, not a bang") | Back to human scale; the avatar reacts |
| 38–45 | SURPRISE + LOOP | Surprising limit, cost or myth-bust, phrased to loop to the opening | Callback to the opening shot with the new understanding (e.g. the label now visible) |

**75 s extension:** add a history-of-the-invention beat (5–8 s), a "wrong way vs right way" comparison, and a second myth-bust; or extend it into a full "how it's made" production line with 5–6 stations.

**Hook templates:**

1. "Every time you [everyday action], [hidden thing] happens [somewhere unseen]."
2. "[Expensive / ordinary product] is made by [violent / strange step]." ("A red hot iron is pushed into the tree, wounding it on purpose." — Zack D, 112.8M)
3. "Have you ever wondered why [body / everyday phenomenon]?" ("Have you ever wondered why water comes out of your eyeballs when you cry?" — Zack D, 42.9M)

Alternates:

- "If you [use X], watch out for these hidden dangers." (Zeck Felms, 16.2M)
- "This is how [X] actually works." (title pattern "How A Gun Silencer Actually Works", 52.5M)

**Best art styles.**

- Stylised 3D with cutaways (strongest evidence)
- Clean 2D infographic/diagram with a stick-figure operator
- Stick figure in a 3D toon machine-world for "factory" stories

**Visual conventions.**

- Exploded view, part labels, flow arrows, macro DOF.
- Show one part at a time; never a whole static diagram.
- Scale bars for tiny or huge parts.
- On-screen numbers.
- Per MakeViral's study of top 3D explainers, aim for about one new detail per second.

**Voice and audio.**

- Calm, precise, faintly amused narrator, 160–190 wpm. Zack D: 157–169 wpm, 28–48 s.
- Music: light curious bed; mechanical SFX (clicks, hiss, whoosh) synced to each part.

**Ending.** "So next time you [action], remember [mechanism]", or a loop line.

**Common mistakes.**

- Starting with history ("invented in 1888…").
- Too many parts.
- Diagrams without motion.
- Technical jargon without an analogy.
- Overlong: 3D how-it-works winners run 28–35 s.

## MODE 4 — HISTORY EXPLAINED (why/how did X happen)

**Toggles:**

- **Dark History:** villain bios, punishments, weird deaths
- **War Tactics:** formations, ambushes, sieges
- **"How to become X"** second-person career path, e.g. "How to be POPE in 55 Years", 21.8M

**Purpose.** Make a weird or surprising historical fact make sense through its causes. Comedy first, accuracy always.

**Input.** An event, custom, ruler, era or weird question ("why did the French hate potatoes", "how did kings avoid poison", "why did soldiers fight in lines").

**Research steps:**

1. Wikipedia plus 2 authoritative sources (Britannica, museum or university pages, a well-cited book).
2. Find the **modern hook angle**: paradox, superlative, or a "you" framing.
3. Lay out the causal chain in 3–5 steps; collect dates, numbers and one primary-source quote if possible.
4. **Myth check.** Popular history Shorts are full of myths; Historically ends one video by busting one: "pilots weren't chained up in their cockpits. That one is a post-war myth."
5. Dark toggle: no gore close-ups; imply rather than show; age-safe wording.

**Script template, 45 s (~155 words at ~205 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | HOOK | Absurd-sounding claim ("Being a king is a very hungry job.") | Character in period costume plus one visual gag (crown + empty plate) |
| 2–5 | BRIDGE | "I know, sounds weird, but hear me out." / "Let me explain." | Character shrug; title card with year |
| 5–15 | STAKES | Why the problem mattered ("everyone kind of just wants to kill you… poison") | Map or establishing shot; threat icons; stamped date |
| 15–30 | ATTEMPTS | 2–3 escalating solutions, each funnier or more extreme (food taster → slow poison → locked kitchens) | One scene per attempt; reaction faces; "FAIL" stamps |
| 30–40 | THE REAL ANSWER / TWIST | The most extreme true fact ("took poison daily to build immunity") | Hero moment: slow zoom, music hit |
| 40–45 | KICKER | Consequence or myth-bust or irony ("…and then the French Revolution started.") + optional "Subscribe." | Ironic final image; loopable |

**75 s extension:** add a "first you have to…" step ladder (the career-path variant), a second era or region comparison, and a myth vs reality split. Historically's winners run 59–95 s at 179–230 wpm.

**Hook templates:**

1. "[People / role] [hated / feared / were obsessed with] [harmless thing]." ("French people hate potatoes, or at least used to." — 21.9M)
2. "Here's how to [become X] in [N years]. First, you have to [absurd prerequisite]." ("…First, you have to be born a man. So, that eliminates about 50% of your chances." — 21.8M)
3. "Why did [soldiers / people] [do obviously dumb thing]?" ("Who were easier to hit, these guys or these funny guys?" — line formations, 20M)

Dark toggle:

- "[Famous person] was way more [evil / gross] than you were taught." ("Christopher Columbus was way more evil than you were taught." — History Dude, 11.3M)
- Title pattern "[Name] Was HORRIFYING!" (Pablo Escobar 21M; Henry VIII 13.3M; Blackbeard 13.2M)

**Best art styles.**

- 2D flat vector characters on maps (Historically)
- Countryballs (7.25M median)
- Cutout caricature heads on oil paintings (History Dude, curiousmotions)
- Stick figures in a 3D period diorama (reference style)

**Visual conventions.**

- Year stamp on every scene change; map establishing shot with a red-arrow/inset circle (Historically).
- Period costume swaps on one recurring character.
- Bold one-word captions ("HERE"); stamps (BANNED, FAILED).
- Troop arrows for tactics; before/after split for myths.

**Voice and audio.**

- Cheeky, conversational, fast (200–230 wpm). Dark toggle: same speed, a lower and drier tone.
- Music: playful pizzicato/baroque; dark toggle uses low strings and a heartbeat pulse.

**Ending.** Ironic kicker, myth-bust, or a one-word "Subscribe." (Historically does this).

**Common mistakes.**

- Textbook chronology.
- Too many dates.
- A neutral biography: "biography" Shorts search median is only 23.5K.
- Repeating internet myths.
- Gore.

## MODE 5 — BACK THEN (everyday life across eras)

**Purpose.** Take one everyday activity or object (shaving, toilets, dentists, dating, food, shoes, entertainment) and time-travel it through 4–6 eras in the second person, ending on a modern punchline that turns on the viewer.

Variants:

- **"Would you survive / get rich…"** — a "Day 1 / Day 2…" business or survival sim in an era. Example: "Would you become rich by selling burgers in ancient Rome?" (4.25M)
- **"How long would [person] survive in every era"** (MrFineWayne, 2.7M)
- **"Origin of [thing]"** — invention story, e.g. Rom "How Nutella Was Invented" (16M)
- **"Ancient humans…"** — the stickman long-form trend ("What Did Ancient Humans Actually Do All Day?", 18M long-form)

**Input.** An everyday thing or activity, or a premise ("selling coffee in ancient Greece").

**Research steps:**

1. Pick 4–6 eras with one concrete, documented method each: Stone Age → Egypt → Rome → Medieval → 1700s → Today. Use 2+ sources per era (museum, university, encyclopedia).
2. For each era: the tool or material, one gross or funny detail, one number.
3. For the sim variant, build a day-by-day ladder: setup → success → historical obstacle (law, plague, tax, material) → twist moral.
4. Myth check. Many "back then" facts online are false — reject anything without a source.

**Script template, 45 s (~150 words at ~200–210 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | "How did people [do X] before [invention] even existed?" | Modern object crossed out; the avatar shrugs |
| 3–12 | ERA 1 | "Stone Age, 100,000 years ago. Your [tool] is a [absurd tool]." + why it mattered ("you're not grooming, you're surviving") | Era title card with rolling year odometer; avatar in costume using the tool |

## MODE 6 — LORE EXPLAINED (anime, manga, games, films, series)

**Purpose.** Make a fan feel they finally understand a rule, power, secret or backstory in their franchise, or give a newcomer the whole plot in 60 s.

Sub-types:

- **Power math / rule explained** (Silenthys: "The math behind Gojo's Infinity", 5.7M)
- **Superlative countdown** (Ohara: "The Strongest Marine In One Piece EVER!", 13.2M)
- **Rating card** (Ohara: "Rating Kizaru's Light Devil Fruit", 7.5M)
- **Plot in 60 s** (Ohara: "One Piece In 60 Seconds", 12M)
- **Backstory / why X turned evil** (search median for "anime villain backstory" is 872K)
- **"In every game"** lists (ITalkFNAF)
- **Hidden-lore reveal** ("Did you know Temple Run might actually have lore?", 12.2M)

**Input.** Franchise plus a question, character, power or arc, plus a spoiler ceiling (e.g. "anime only" / "up to ch. 1100").

**Research steps:**

1. Use the official wiki (Fandom), chapter/episode citations, official databooks and creator interviews. Separate canon from theory.
2. Check the **latest episode or chapter** for timeliness. Release weeks spike demand; StojDoes and Silenthys uploads land during airing.
3. For power math, compute the numbers explicitly. Example: Black Flash = damage to the power of 2.5, "A punch worth 10 becomes 300."
4. Prepare a spoiler warning if it goes beyond the anime.
5. **IP safety:**
   - No clips, screenshots, official art or music.
   - Use original stand-ins: silhouettes or stick figures with signature props and colours (straw hat, blindfold), plus original diagrams.
   - Rights-holder enforcement on YouTube, TikTok and IG is intensifying in 2026.

**Script template, 45 s (~165 words at ~215 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | Paradox or superlative ("The strongest punch in Jujutsu Kaisen is the one nobody can do on purpose.") | Character stand-in in action pose; title card with the power name |
| 3–10 | DEFINE | What the thing is, in one line, plus its rule | Character card (name, faction colour, rank); rule written as an equation or diagram |
| 10–25 | MECHANICS | 2–3 escalating facts with numbers or comparisons ("within 1 millionth of a second… sprinters need a tenth…") | Number counters, scale comparisons, timeline bars |
| 25–35 | TWIST | The canon detail most fans missed, or the countdown reveal ("…but it's none of the Admirals") | Silhouette reveal; manga-style speed lines; source stamp ("Ch. 1093") |
| 35–45 | PAYOFF + PROMPT | Final number, score, or verdict + debate question / "Follow for more [franchise] explained" | Score meter filling, or a versus split; comment prompt text |

**75 s extension:** plot-in-60 (one beat per 3–4 s, 15–18 beats), or a full rating card. Rating card layout: power 25 + versatility 25 + awakening 25 + weakness deduction → "a whopping 35 points" / "82 out of 100… number one for now".

**Hook templates:**

1. "The [strongest / most broken] [thing] in [franchise] is the one [paradox]." (Silenthys, 2.1M; also "the fastest way to get stronger is to make yourself weaker", 1.2M)
2. "This is the strongest [rank] in [franchise] of all time, and it's someone no one would have expected." (Ohara, 13.2M)
3. "I'm rating every [power / devil fruit / weapon] in [franchise] to figure out which one's the best." (Ohara, 7.5M)

Also:

- "Did you know [franchise] actually has [hidden lore]?" (Rocket Riley "Did you know Earth actually exists in Star Wars canon?", 12.3M)
- "What is the scariest story in the [franchise] books?" (ITalkFNAF, 7.1M)

**Best art styles.**

- Stick-figure / silhouette stand-ins with iconic props in a stylised arena
- Game-UI stat cards and meters (Ohara)
- Infographic/diagram for power math
- 2D cel-shaded original characters only if clearly not copies

**Visual conventions.**

- Character card on first mention.
- Faction colour coding.
- Numbers always visualised.
- Countdown elimination: cross out each wrong guess.
- Spoiler banner.
- Rating meter at the end.

**Voice and audio.**

- Hype fan energy, fast (195–250 wpm; FNAF lore channels hit 250–283).
- Music: original epic/trap-orchestral; impact hits on reveals.

**Ending.** Score or verdict plus a debate prompt, or a "follow for more" CTA (Silenthys ends: "Follow Silenthys for more anime explained").

**Common mistakes.**

- Using clips or official art (claims and strikes).
- Wrong canon (fans correct you in the comments).
- Spoilers without warning.
- Slow pace.
- Overly long recap with no twist.

## MODE 7 — MAP EXPLAINED (geography, borders, countries)

**Purpose.** Make a map oddity make sense through 3–5 historical moves: a weird border, a long or thin country, a divided island, an exclave, a land purchase, a region name, a language split. The map is the stage, so this is the cheapest mode to render in code (SVG/GeoJSON) and highly re-usable.

**Input.** A place plus its oddity ("Why is Chile so long?", "Why Cyprus is divided", "Why Bolivia owns this tiny triangle").

**Research steps:**

1. Wikipedia, CIA World Factbook, and treaty or official sources. Get real coordinates/GeoJSON for borders from Natural Earth (public domain).
2. Causal chain: 3–5 dated events, each one change in map state (annex, treaty, war, purchase, partition).
3. One surprising number (e.g. "$15 million… 4 cents an acre").
4. Neutrality pass for live conflicts: give sourced facts and both names for disputed places. Avoid current-war takes; Instagram down-ranks political content.

**Script template, 45 s (~145 words at ~190 wpm):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | "This is [place]. But did you know [oddity]?" | Map zoom from globe to the place; oddity highlighted (pulsing outline) |
| 3–10 | SHOW THE ODDITY | Make it concrete (size, shape, who owns what) | Colour fills, labels, inset photo circle, scale bar |
| 10–32 | CAUSAL CHAIN | 3 moves at about 7 s each ("1802…", "then yellow fever…", "so Napoleon needed money…") | Each move = a border redraw, troop/ship arrows and a year stamp; countryball or stick-figure leaders react |
| 32–40 | TWIST | The irony ("they were only authorised to buy one city") | Slam zoom; "?!" reaction; a number counter |
| 40–45 | SUMMARY KICKER | "And that's how [place] ended up [state]…" + a number | Final map state; loop to the opening framing |

**75–100 s extension:** 5 moves plus the present-day consequence (identity, culture, disputes). GeoGlobeTales routinely runs 85–103 s and still gets 5–18.6M, because each map change is a mini-reveal.

**Hook templates:**

1. "This is [country]. But did you know [weird fact]?" (GeoGlobeTales, Gibraltar 6.7M; geoalldayog, Asia regions 2.5M)
2. "[Country] offered to buy just [small thing], but somehow ended up buying [huge thing]." (Louisiana, 18.6M)
3. "[Place] has some really weird borders." (Historically, USA borders, 16.5M) / "Why does [country] own this tiny [shape]?" (geoalldayog, Bolivia triangle, 7.3M)

Also: "Have you ever wondered why [word / language quirk]?" — e.g. "…why the meat of cows… is called beef… and only chicken is still just chicken?" (9.5M).

**Best art styles.**

- Flat vector map (Historically) or satellite-style relief map (GeoGlobeTales)
- Countryballs as characters (KingCountryballAnimations median 7.25M; MrSpherical 441K)
- Stick-figure diplomats walking on a 3D globe

**Visual conventions.**

- The map never disappears.
- Every claim is a map-state change: fill, border, arrow or pin.
- Year stamp, inset photo circles, red arrow pointers.
- Ranking variant ("Widest Countries", 24M): a sorted bar of country silhouettes.

**Voice and audio.**

- Friendly, curious explainer at 185–195 wpm.
- Light orchestral/world music; a whoosh for each zoom.

**Ending.** "And that's how/why…" summary plus a kicker number or irony ("…stand in America, look at Russia, and see tomorrow.").

**Common mistakes.**

- Static maps.
- Too many place names.
- Political editorialising.
- Wrong borders: always use real data.

## MODE 8 — PROBLEM → SOLUTION (app / product marketing)

**Purpose.** Deliver a genuinely useful explainer about the viewer's problem (why it happens and how to fix it). The product appears late, in-world, as the easy way to apply the fix. This mode optimises for downloads, not views, but **views drive downloads**, so it must stand on its own as content.

**Input (required):**

- Product name and what it does
- Target user
- The user's core problem
- 2–3 real features that map to the fix
- Proof points (only true ones)
- CTA text and link destination
- Brand colour/logo
- Forbidden claims (medical/financial/legal)

**Research steps:**

1. Find the mechanism behind the problem. Examples: variable rewards / Skinner box for doomscrolling; sleep pressure and blue light for insomnia; the spacing effect for forgetting.
2. Get 1–2 credible sources or stats for the mechanism.
3. Mine the user's own words from reviews, Reddit and comments. Use them in the hook and the relatable scene.
4. Map one fix → one product feature; prepare an honest phrasing.
5. Compliance pass: no guaranteed outcomes, no fake testimonials, disclose sponsorship where required.

**Script template, 45 s (~150 words):**

| t (s) | Beat | Script job | On screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | HOOK | Name the viewer's pain + a surprise ("Why you can't stop scrolling — and it's not your fault.") | "You" avatar in the relatable moment (2 a.m., phone glow); bold 2-word caption |
| 3–12 | MECHANISM | The hidden cause, explained simply (Skinner-box experiment → slot machine → feed) | Diagram or cutaway: brain/reward loop, lab rat lever, slot reels morphing into a feed |
| 12–22 | CONSEQUENCE | What it costs you, with a number (hours, sleep, money) | Clock or counter spinning; avatar exhausted |
| 22–32 | THE FIX | 1 actionable, value-first principle (break the loop: friction, schedules, replacement reward) | The avatar applies the fix in-world; before/after split |
| 32–41 | PRODUCT AS TOOL | "The easiest way to do this is [product]: it [feature mapped to the fix]." | The app appears as a diegetic device in the toon world (phone or object in the avatar's hand); a 2–3 s real UI insert is allowed |
| 41–45 | SOFT CTA + LOOP | One-line soft CTA ("[Product], link in bio") + a callback to the hook moment, now solved | Hook scene replayed, resolved; small logo end card |

Placement rule: the product is introduced after about 70% of runtime. Operator evidence:

- Jake Castillo (Cal AI co-founder): put the app on slide 4–5 of 5–10, "let the story earn the attention first", and optimise for views.
- facelessfarms: the best campaign content kept the app mention light and put the CTA in the caption.
- rossark0: app icon around the middle or end.

**75 s extension.** Use 2 mechanisms or a myth-bust, a 3-step fix (one step uses the product), and a mini-case ("I tried this for 7 days…") before the CTA.

**Hook templates:**

1. "Why you can't [stop X / sleep / focus] (it's not your fault)." (G Logics "Why You Can't Stop Scrolling: The Skinner Box Explained", 26.7M)
2. "[Authority] [credential]. These are the [N] [rules / tips] [specific outcome]." (facelessfarms: "My dad worked homicide for 30 years. These are the rules he made every girl in our family memorize." — 80M views across a $40k campaign)
3. "How to trick your brain into [desired behaviour]." (Zack D "How To Trick Your Brain To Stop Procrastinating", 6.4M)

Also:

- "[Condition] explained in 60 seconds." (21.8M)
- "Your [brain / body] does [weird thing] every time you [action]."

**Best art styles.**

- The SAME recurring avatar character as the brand's organic channel (series recognition)
- Stick figure in a 3D toon world, or flat 2D
- Brain/body cutaways
- The app UI only as a short insert

**Visual conventions.**

- Problem scene → mechanism diagram → consequence counter → fix in-world → product in-hand → resolved callback.
- Never open on the logo or the UI.

**Voice and audio.**

- Empathetic peer, not an announcer; about 190–210 wpm.
- Music goes from tense or lo-fi to uplifting at the fix.

**Benchmarks to set expectations:**

- Influencer UGC: about 5 downloads per 1,000 views (Cal AI benchmark).
- Slideshow farms: about 1 download per 2,000 views.
- About 90% of slideshows stay under 5K views. Volume and multiple accounts matter (Jake Castillo, Oct 2026).

**Common mistakes.**

- Making a video ABOUT the app ("nobody wants to watch an ad" — Peef).
- A hard CTA on screen.
- The product appearing in the first half.
- Unverifiable health or money claims.
- Generic advice with no mechanism.
- Posting once a day from one account (volume is the lever).

**Overall:**

**Shot rate.**

- Storyboard sampling at 1 fps on Hoodie Guy "A Horrifying Parasite…": visual change in 61% of 1-second intervals, so an implied mean shot length of ≤1.6 s.
- Secondary sources agree:
  - About one new detail per second in top 3D explainers (MakeViral).
  - Cuts every 2–4 s in Zack-D-style pipelines.
  - Stickman channels: change every 2–3 s, never hold a picture over 2.5 s (varg.ai stickman director).
- **Rule for the renderer:** a new shot, element or camera move every 1–2.5 s; a hard cut on every new noun phrase in the hook.

## 4.6 Method and caveats

**YouTube Shorts search.**

- About 140 keyword queries, scraped from the public Shorts search page (Shorts filter, relevance order, 2–4 result pages each).
- About 3,800 Shorts with approximate view counts.
- Channel names came from the oEmbed endpoint.
- Publish dates were fetched for 646 of them before YouTube rate-limited watch pages.
- Of the dated top results, 437/646 (68%) were 2025–26 uploads.

**Channels.** 104 channels profiled from their /shorts tabs (yt-dlp flat playlist, newest first, 60 per channel).

**Captions.** 150 + 12 Shorts pulled through yt-dlp (web_embedded client, captions and metadata only, no video files); 97 had usable English captions. These gave the verbatim hooks, endings, WPM and lengths.

**Caveats.**

- View counts are YouTube's rounded figures (e.g. "1.2M").
- Search results are relevance-ranked, not a random sample.
- Channel medians reflect the best operators, not the typical channel.
- TikTok and Instagram could not be measured directly (their logged-out pages don't expose stats). TikTok and IG guidance comes from secondary sources: TikTok Next 2026 ("Curiosity Detours": 2 in 3 searchers discover beyond their query), the Creator Rewards ≥1-minute rule, MakeViral, and the X operator posts. Most of these leading channels cross-post the same videos to TikTok, IG and Facebook (e.g. Zack D: about 10.6–11M Instagram followers, 15.6M on Facebook).
- Vendor blog figures (Taletok, MakeViral, Virvid, Anijam) are directional only.
- X claims are self-reported.
- Shorts views have counted every play or replay since 31 Mar 2025, which inflates looped short videos.
